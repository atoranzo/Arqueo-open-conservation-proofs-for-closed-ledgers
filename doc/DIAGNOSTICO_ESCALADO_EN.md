## 0.bis WHAT WAS MEASURED (§204) — read it before the rest

This document was written **before** measuring. Afterwards five things
were measured, and **three of its hypotheses fell**. They are kept
written further down with their refutation beside them —it is the house
rule— but the current state is this:

| bench | question | result |
|---|---|---|
| `etapa_a_apply` | where do the ms of `apply` go? | persistence **3 %** · `apply` = **37,99 ms** → **26,3 op/s** (not 5,7 TPS) |
| `etapa_a2_verify` | verification or trees? | verification **7 %** · the rest **93 %** |
| `etapa_a3_escala` | does it grow with the accounts? | `apply` **e = 0,18** (flat) · `send_materials` **e = 1,08** |
| `etapa_a4_hash` | what is that 93 %? | **`digest_of_proof`: 30,99 ms, 2.915× Blake3** |
| `etapa_a5_concurrencia` | does the contention exist? | **3,83 regenerations/payment**, 66 % of the work thrown away, 0,84× when parallelized |

**The three refuted hypotheses:** the `flush` per operation (§6.4.ter
level 0), the `nonce` as anti-replay (§5), and the quadratic tree.

**The two confirmed, and what each one fixes:**

| fix | fixes | does NOT fix |
|---|---|---|
| `digest_of_proof` Rescue → non-algebraic hash | the node's ceiling: **30 → 436 op/s** | the contention (the 66 % of waste stays) |
| decoupling the three roots | claims dying from other people's sends (**4,1×** worse than sends) | sends among themselves |
| batches | the whole contention | the ceiling, if the hash stays |
| incremental tree | the collapse of `send_materials` at scale | none of the above |

**None works alone.** The benches are in `crates/zk-ssl/examples/` and
the five complete tables, in `AUDITORIA.md` §204.

⚠️ What this document claimed and **no longer holds**: that the group
commit was «the cheap multiple» (persistence is 3 %), and that one had
to choose between «real contention» and «lack of parallelism» (they are
**both** at once).

## 0. The case that motivated it

In August 2026 an external scaling diagnosis was checked against this
repository. It recommended, in short: delegating proof generation to a
GPU cluster because of the saturation of **MSM and NTT**; partitioning
the **nullifier registry**; isolating the `settlement-prover` crate as
a microservice; and aggregating with **Nova or Halo2**.

It is standard and correct advice **for a rollup over elliptic
curves**. Against this tree, four of the five recommendations point at
things that do not exist here, and one would destroy the system's
central property.

The episode is recorded, not the embarrassment: **the lesson is that a
document that only says where the limit is invites inventing the
others**.

## 1. The real bottleneck, measured

| | |
|---|---|
| **Contention of the root anchoring** | **1,5-1,9 TPS** — `AUDITORIA.md` §123, backlog entry 65 |
| Ceiling of the sequential `apply` | ~5,7 TPS (`doc/ESCALADO.md` §2.1) |
| Accumulation of proofs | ~124 MiB per thousand transfers |

Each proof is tied to the **exact root** it saw when it was generated:
that is the current anti-replay. The root changes every ~177 ms and
generating costs hundreds of ms, so under concurrency almost no proof
arrives alive and the effective throughput collapses with cascading
regenerations.

**That is the limit that bites first, and it is not one of compute: it
is one of the structure of the anti-replay.**

### 1.1 Three limitations coupled to the same bottleneck, not written until now

**1.1.a — The contention is not of ONE root: there are THREE, and they are coupled.**
`apply_send` requires `root_old`, `pending_root_old` **and**
`frozen_root` to match (`two_phase.rs`). Consequence: **a governance
freeze —which has no relation whatsoever to the payments in flight—
invalidates them all.** The contention is not generated by the payments
alone.

**1.1.b — A payment is TWO operations, so the TPS target doubles.**
`Send` and `Claim` are separate log entries, each with its proof and
its root transition (`spec/vectors/`: `… Send →
Claim`). Any target expressed in «payments per second» must be
**doubled** to express it in layer operations. See §6.2.

**1.1.c — Finality depends on the receiver.** The money stays in the
pending tree until someone **claims**. For a settlement system this is
a serious semantic mismatch: in an RTGS finality is immediate at the
originator's act. Here it depends on the receiver acting **and** on the
notice reaching them out of band (§3.2). Expiry and refund (#27/#28)
prevent the money from being lost, but **they do not make the payment
final**: they return it.

## 2. What is NOT the bottleneck

### 2.1 There is no MSM and no elliptic curves on the production path

The backend is **STARK/FRI over Goldilocks** (`winterfell`). The cost
of proving is **hash + FFT**, not multi-scalar multiplication.

```bash
grep -rn "msm\|multi_scalar\|G1Projective\|pairing" crates/stark-experiment/src/
# → 0 coincidencias
```

The curves live **only** in the experiment crates of the comparative
study (`zk-core` with BLS12-381, `halo2-experiment` with Pallas,
`plonk-experiment`, `nova-experiment`), isolated on purpose and **off
the payment path**. Optimizing MSM here is tuning an engine that is not
mounted.

### 2.2 The node does not generate proofs — the client generates them

```bash
grep -rn "pub fn prove_send\|pub fn prove_claim" crates/zk-ssl/src/client.rs
```

`prove_send` and `prove_claim` run **locally, on the holder**. The node
hands over materials —paths and roots, public data— and **verifies**.

⚠️ That is why «delegating the proving to an external cluster» **is not
an optimization, it is a security regression**: the witness contains
material derived from the spending key, so a cluster that receives
witnesses receives the keys. It is exactly the property the system
upholds (`Wallet::spend_key` does not even implement `Serialize`).

And besides **it is not needed**: `doc/ESCALADO.md` §3.1 already
measures that generation is the client's and therefore **scales by
itself with the users** —~1,24 s/day per device at two payments a day—.
Aggregate proving capacity grows with adoption; it is the only part of
the system that needs no scaling plan.

### 2.3 The nullifier registry does not exist

The one-step path was **withdrawn with its nullifier tree**
(`AUDITORIA.md` §32 and §36). The production path is the two-phase one,
with a **pending** tree and anti-replay by root chaining.

```bash
grep -c "nullifier" crates/zk-ssl-wire/src/lib.rs   # → 0
grep -c "pending"   crates/zk-ssl-wire/src/lib.rs   # → 23
```

Partitioning it, caching it or *sharding* it is work on a withdrawn
structure. *(That same error survived inside the repository itself
until §203: four documents still presented its limit as current. It is
not an exotic error — it is easy to make.)*

### 2.4 `settlement-prover` is not a proving service

It exists, and it is a **91-line trait deliberately without
dependencies**: the interface that lets compliance-proof backends be
compared without Arkworks and Halo2 colliding. **Neither `zk-ssl`, nor
the node, nor the SDK uses it.** There is no prover to isolate on GPUs;
there is an abstraction of the comparative study.

### 2.5 The ledger does not grow with the proofs

`LogEntry` stores **the digest of the proof, not the proof** (`log.rs`).
One entry is ~137 bytes.

```bash
grep -n "no la prueba entera" crates/zk-ssl/src/log.rs
```

Any scaling plan that begins with «the node's proof history must be
compressed» is solving a problem the node does not have. What is
missing is **a retention policy for third parties** (§6.3).

## 3. The two dodges, declared for what they are

None of these problems is **solved**. They are **avoided**, and what
avoids them is having **a single node**. Whoever distributes the state
gets them back whole.

**3.1 Total order.** Anti-replay by root chaining requires that someone
put the operations in an order. A single node gives it for free; a
distributed system does not. It replaced the nullifier marker —and with
it its collision at ~65.000 payments— but **it traded a limit for a
requirement**.

**3.2 Client synchronization.** In systems with private notes the
receiver cannot know they have been paid without scanning all the
traffic —the classic usability problem of private coins—. Here it does
not happen because the `PendingNotice` **travels out of band** from the
payer to the receiver (`spec/RPC.md`, debt §21; ⚠️ §861: the entry of
the side channel is §39; §21 deals with the reason codes).
Synchronization cost: zero.

⚠️ **But the cost did not disappear: it moved from the cryptography to
the product**, and its consequences are not measured:

- the out-of-band channel is an **unaudited dependency**, and if it
  leaks who talks to whom, it leaks the payment graph the proofs
  protect;
- **ISO 20022 does not carry it** (`spec/RPC.md`), so the institutional
  bridge has a gap through which the datum that makes the payment
  claimable must pass (⚠️ §861: «does not carry it» is not exact: there
  is no field made for the notice; the extension one, `SplmtryData`,
  admits it (read in a model derived from the XSDs, not checked against
  the original); using the remittance one would be forcing it; the
  channel, decided by delegation in entry 11 of `BACKLOG.md`, is still
  unbuilt, and the bridge's gap stays there until then);
- **unsolicited payments do not exist**: one cannot pay someone with
  whom one has no channel. In a regulated context it is almost a virtue;
  as money, it is a real limitation.

If the notice is lost, the money **is not trapped**: expiry and refund
return it (circuits #27 and #28, `AUDITORIA.md` §178-§180).

## 4. What is ruled out on principle, not for difficulty

**Consensus of its own.** `SECURITY.md` §6 holds that consensus does
not remove the intermediary: it makes it plural and expensive.
Implementing it and calling that «decentralized» would contradict the
repository itself. And a BFT written by a single person and unaudited
would trade **an honest, declared weakness for a false strength**: its
failures —liveness under partition, equivocation, view change— are not
tested with discriminating witnesses, which is the only verification
method this house has.

**Aggregation with Nova or Halo2.** They were measured and ruled out
for reintroducing ceremony and losing quantum resistance. It is the
only decision of the project taken **against** the performance numbers;
recovering them through the back door would annul it. If one day
aggregation is needed, the coherent path is **FRI recursion**, without
curves.

**Delegated proving.** See §2.2. It is not a question of cost.

**The step that IS coherent**, and is already designed without being
built: attested heads and **acknowledgement** (§121,
`doc/CONFIANZA_RESIDUAL.md`), external anchoring of roots
(`doc/ANCLAJE_EXTERNO.md`) and verifiable replication. They do not
share the order out among peers: they make it so that **the operator
cannot lie without leaving evidence**. It can be tested with a
discriminating test —the operator omits, the acknowledgement detects
it—, it fits in one person, and it demands no new claim.

## 5. The `nonce` line: HYPOTHESIS REFUTED, and what does survive

> An earlier version of this document proposed hanging the anti-replay
> from the account `nonce` as «the only line with an advantage». **It
> was measured and it does not work.** The refutation is left written,
> the idea is not erased: it is what saves the attempt for whoever comes
> next.

### 5.1 What the code says

Two facts, checkable:

```bash
sed -n '554,568p' crates/zk-ssl/src/two_phase.rs   # apply_send
grep -n "El nonce NO cambia" crates/stark-experiment/src/circuit_send.rs
```

1. `apply_send` ties **three global roots at once** —`root_old`,
   `pending_root_old` and `frozen_root`—: any change in any of the
   three returns `StaleState` and kills **all** the proofs in flight.
2. **The `nonce` does not even advance with the payment**:
   `circuit_send` says so in its own comment —*«el nonce NO cambia:
   destruir no consume el derecho»* [the nonce does NOT change:
   destroying does not consume the right]—. Today it is not an
   operation counter.

### 5.2 Why the idea fails, and not because of the `nonce`

The obstacle is **below** the anti-replay: the proof carries a **Merkle
path against `root_old`** and also **asserts `root_new`**. Even if the
anti-replay hung from the `nonce`, the path is still against a root
that any other account can change.

And removing the root tie forces the reintroduction of **a double-spend
marker** — that is, the nullifier tree withdrawn in §32/§36, with its
collision at ~65.000 payments. **The idea bites its own tail.**

### 5.3 The reformulation that DOES survive, with its price

That the client prove **only the transition of its leaf** —plus
membership against `root_old`— and **NOT assert `root_new`**; that the
node apply a **batch** of leaf transitions and recompute the root once.
Then N proofs against the same root **compose**, and the contention
goes from being per operation to being per batch.

And here the `nonce` **does gain a real job**: deduplicating within the
batch —one operation per account and per batch—, which is just what the
global chaining did for free.

⚠️ **The price.** Today **every root transition is proven**. With
batches, the tree update would be done by the node **without a proof**.

### 5.3.bis How to pay that price WITHOUT recursion

> An earlier version of this section took the price as inevitable and
> referred to recursion —which §6.4 declares infeasible—. **That was
> badly reasoned pessimism**: the property is not lost, it is shared
> out.

Verification splits into two **independent** claims, which do not
require verifying any proof *inside* a circuit:

| who | what it proves |
|---|---|
| **each client** | it knows its key, its old leaf belongs to `root_old`, and this is its new leaf. **It does not assert `root_new`** |
| **the node, once per batch** | applying these N new leaves at these N positions over `root_old` yields `root_new` — plus the conservation of the batch's supply |

A verifier checks **N client proofs + 1 batch proof**, and together
they assert **exactly the same as a single one asserts today**.

**This is not recursion**: nobody verifies a proof inside a circuit;
**independent claims are composed**. The batch circuit is N Merkle
ascents in one trace — large, but of the same nature as the two ascents
the current circuits already do. And the `nonce` plays its role here:
one operation per account and per batch.

**What must be measured before believing it** (and this section has
already had to be corrected once, so the reservation is serious):

1. Trace length and **RAM** for N = 50 / 100 / 500. It may blow up
   sooner than intuition suggests.
2. That **supply conservation** is proven at batch level, not at
   operation level.
3. That the **three** trees (accounts, pending, frozen) fit in the same
   batch without serializing again (§1.1.a).
4. The **batch proving time**, which becomes the new ceiling: if
   proving 100 costs 10 s, that is ~10 TPS and not much has been gained.

### 5.4 What is still unmeasured

- Whether **supply conservation** survives a partial order.
- What happens to the pending tree and to the epoch head when two
  accounts advance with no order between them.

Until that is measured, §5.3 is **a direction, not a plan**.

## 6. The RTGS target: what is really missing, and what is not cryptography

This section exists because the question «could this serve the FED or
the ECB?» is almost always answered with the wrong intuition. The
numbers here are external and **carry source and date at the foot**.

### 6.1 The framing correction: an RTGS has no consensus

**TARGET2 and Fedwire are not decentralized systems.** A central bank
operates them; the trusted operator **is** the institution. Therefore:

> The single node of this project **is not disqualifying for this use
> case**. Its residual-trust model —the operator orders and sees the
> state— **matches** the real RTGS model instead of contradicting it.

What is disqualifying is something else, and it is not cryptographic:
**high availability, disaster recovery, audited operational
continuity, certification and legal finality of settlement**.

Practical corollary, consistent with `SECURITY.md` §6: **pursuing
consensus would move away from the institutional target instead of
towards it.** What brings it closer is that the operator **cannot lie
without leaving evidence**.

### 6.2 The arithmetic, which is better than intuition suggests

| | |
|---|---|
| Fedwire Funds, average daily volume (2024) | **836.322 operations** |
| Hours | 22 h per business day |
| **Implied average** | **~10,6 TPS** |
| Estimated peak (end-of-day concentration, 3-5× the average) | **~30-50 TPS** |
| Arqueo today, under concurrency | **1,5-1,9 TPS** (§1) |

⚠️ **Correction: a payment is TWO layer operations** (§1.1.b). The real
target is not 10,6 TPS but **~21 TPS on average** (~1,67 M
operations/day), and the estimated peak rises to **~60-105 TPS**.

**The gap is ~11× on average and ~30-55× at peak.** It is still not
three orders of magnitude —it is engineering distance, not physics— but
it is **twice what an earlier version of this section said**.

### 6.3 Storage is NOT the disqualifier — and an earlier version of this document said it was

> **Measured correction.** This section claimed that at Fedwire volume
> the system would accumulate ~36 TiB/year of proofs, and called it
> «the true technical disqualifier». **It is false**, and reading the
> code is enough to see it. It is corrected and the error is put on
> record, which is the house rule.

**The layer persists digests, not proofs.** `crates/zk-ssl/src/log.rs`,
in the definition of `LogEntry`, says it literally: *«Resumen de la
prueba, no la prueba entera. Guardar las pruebas completas serían ~62 KB
por operación… El resumen basta para atar la entrada a una prueba
concreta, y quien quiera verificarla puede pedirla.»* [Digest of the
proof, not the whole proof. Storing the complete proofs would be ~62 KB
per operation… The digest is enough to tie the entry to a concrete
proof, and whoever wants to verify it can ask for it.]

One log entry is `seq + kind + root_old + root_new +
proof_digest + chain` ≈ **137 bytes**. At Fedwire volume:

| | |
|---|---|
| Log growth | **~114 MB/day** · **~42 GB/year** |

*(836.322 × 137 B. Estimate from the structure, not measured on disk.)*

The ~124 MiB per thousand payments are **proof bytes generated and in
transit**, not ledger growth. The node does not store them.

⚠️ **What does remain open, and was not said anywhere**: if nobody
keeps the proofs, **re-verification by a third party** depends on the
producer keeping its own. It is a real gap —of retention policy, not of
terabytes— and today it has neither a policy nor a section of its own.
Whoever wants the log to be re-verifiable by third parties in perpetuity
has to decide **who archives, for how long and with what guarantee**.

### 6.4 The three levers, with a feasibility verdict

> An earlier version listed them as if all three were available.
> **Measured, one is done, one is infeasible today and one only
> survives reformulated and with a price.** This table is the result.

| Lever | Can it be done? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Anti-replay by `nonce`** | ❌ as it was formulated · ⚠️ **yes, reformulated** | The obstacle is not the `nonce` but the Merkle path against `root_old` and the assertion of `root_new` (§5.2). Reformulated —leaf-transition proof + batch on the node— it is feasible and falls within the project's domain, **at the cost of the tree update no longer being proven** (§5.3) |
| **2. FRI recursion / batches** | ❌ **not with these resources** | `winterfell` **brings no recursive verifier** (`grep -c recursi` over the crate: 0). Writing a STARK verifier as an AIR is research-level work —Miden built it on Winterfell with a team and years—. Adopting Plonky3 or Miden would break the criterion of auditable dependencies and the same one that ruled out RISC Zero for requiring an external toolchain. **And its declared motivation was false** (§6.3) |
| **3. Client-side generation** | ✅ **already achieved** | `client.rs`; ~1,24 s/day per device (`doc/ESCALADO.md` §3.1). ⚠️ With a reservation: under contention, the **cascading regenerations multiply the client's work**, so it scales in aggregate and **degrades per user under load**. It is coupled to lever 1 |

**Honest reading of the table**: of the three levers this document
presented as a path, **one direction remains** (1 reformulated), **one
closed door** (2) and **one box already ticked** (3). The ceiling of
1,5-1,9 TPS **has no cheap path today**, and saying so is more useful
than keeping three arrows on a diagram.

### 6.5 The engineering ladder, in order

*(None of these steps depends on levers 1 and 2: they are eligibility,
not performance. They can be done today.)*

1. **Encrypt the node's ledger.** The primitive exists
   (`zk_ssl::crypto`, `open_encrypted`); the wiring is missing. One
   seal. `SECURITY.md` §3.3.
2. **Authentication, TLS and rate limiting on the RPC.** Today only the
   bind to `127.0.0.1` protects it. `SECURITY.md` §3.3.
3. **The `PendingNotice` and the standard.** Today it travels out of
   band and `spec/RPC.md` declares that ISO 20022 does not carry it —
   while Fedwire completed its migration to ISO 20022 in July 2025. It
   is a **specification** problem, with a channel: an RFC in
   `spec/rfc/`. ⚠️ §861: `spec/RPC.md` no longer declares it so: it
   carries its mark, and the extension field, `SplmtryData`, admits it.
   The channel is decided: an RFC that specifies the notice in that
   field or outside the message (entry 11 of `BACKLOG.md`), still
   unwritten.
4. **Verifiable replica and acknowledgement** (§121,
   `doc/CONFIANZA_RESIDUAL.md`): that a participant verify the history
   without having to believe the operator. **This is what an
   institution really asks for**, and it is not consensus.
5. **Real disaster recovery**: hot replica, measured recovery point,
   documented restoration exercise.

### 6.6 What is not engineering, and so no seal solves it

Legal finality of settlement (Settlement Finality Directive in the EU,
Regulation J in the US), operational certification, operator governance
and audited continuity. **That only exists inside an institution.** It
cannot be built from a repository; one can only be **eligible**.

### 6.7 The strategy that follows from all the above

The path is **not** to scale until it looks like Fedwire. It is to
reach a **narrow pilot** —a supervisor, a small bank, a consortium of
three— where ~1,9 TPS and an honest operator are *enough*, because this
project's argument is not performance:

> privacy **with** provable supervision, **without a trust ceremony**
> and **post-quantum today** — just when the post-quantum migration is
> open as an agenda problem at the BIS and at both central banks
> (`SECURITY.md` §3.ter).

TPS is solved afterwards, with resources, and the arithmetic of §6.2
says it is reachable. What §6.6 covers is not solved without the
institution. **That is why the right order is: eligibility first,
performance after.**

### 6.4.ter How the gap is closed: four levels and a prior measurement

The ~11× gap **is not one problem, it is two**: ×3 of waste from
contention (1,9 → 5,7) and ×4 of `apply` ceiling (5,7 → 21). They are
not attacked the same way.

**Level 0 — ✅ DONE (§204), and it refuted its own premise.** This
section said: «if the dominant cost is I/O, there is the cheapest
multiple». It was measured: **persistence is 3 %**. The `flush` per
operation exists, but it costs 0,55 ms out of 38. **The premise of
level 1 was false.**

What did turn out to be the 93 %: `digest_of_proof`, an algebraic hash
over the whole proof for a digest no circuit uses (§0.bis).

| level | what | touches circuits | expected gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · layer** | ⚠️ **the group commit is RULED OUT** (persistence 3 %). Instead: **change `digest_of_proof`** (×14 of the ceiling) and **decouple the three trees** (§1.1.a, measured in §204: claims die 4,1× more). Parallel verification yields little: it is 7 % | no (the hash does touch the **wire**: RFC) | **×14** the node's ceiling |
| **2 · circuits** | **batches** (§5.3.bis) · **aggregated claim**: N pending notes in ONE operation | yes | ×3 (contention) and ×2 (a payment stops costing two operations) |
| **3 · architecture** | **partition by account range, with a single operator**. The two-phase design **is already the protocol between partitions**: `send` debits and emits the note in the payer's partition, `claim` consumes it in the receiver's. The note is the atomic commitment | yes | almost linear in the number of partitions, **without consensus** |
| **4 · domain** | **netting and liquidity saving**: what TARGET2 and Fedwire really use — netting cycles instead of settling gross at peak | no | reduces the volume to settle from the other side |

⚠️ **The multipliers do NOT compose cleanly**: each level uncovers a new
ceiling. The reasonable reading is that **the ~21 TPS average looks
reachable with levels 1 and 2**, that the **peak of 63-106 needs level
3**, and that level 4 lowers the bar from the other end.

**The detailed work plan, in five stages with their measurements and
their gates, is written in
[`spec/rfc/0002-lotes-y-transicion-de-hoja.md`](../spec/rfc/en/0002-lotes-y-transicion-de-hoja.md)**
(status DRAFT).

And what does not change: **none of this fixes eligibility** (§6.5,
§6.6). TPS is the easy problem of the two.

### 6.4.bis If one day recursion were needed: Plonky3 vs Miden

The analysis is left done so as not to repeat it, with the conclusion
up front: **today neither of the two is needed**, because the batch
route (§5.3.bis) needs no recursion.

**The comparison is badly posed from the start**: Miden VM **uses
Plonky3 as its proof system**. They are not alternatives — Miden runs
on Plonky3. The real choice is **AIR library** versus **virtual
machine**.

**Miden VM — ❌ ruled out, and a finding of the project's own decides
it.** It is a zkVM: exactly the category this project ruled out when it
evaluated RISC Zero (finding 8: external toolchain, 3 dependencies
against 349). Adopting it would contradict a published finding. And the
larger cost is not that: the circuits would become **programs**, so
that the **executable ESPEC, the cell census and the guardian of 28
circuits —the whole FV ladder (§195-§196)— would stop applying**.
Besides, its recursion is still announced as upcoming, not available.

**Plonky3 — ⚠️ the only technically feasible one, with three costs.**
Same AIR paradigm as Winterfell, so the migration is AIR→AIR; the Miden
team itself publicly documented that migration from Winterfell, which
proves the path is passable. It is an audited toolkit, and
`Plonky3-recursion` offers native recursive verification. The costs:

1. **It breaks a measured differentiator of this project**: «3
   dependencies against 349» is finding 8. Plonky3 is many crates.
2. **Fine collision with finding 4**: the recursion library warns that
   **field extensions are not yet fully parameterizable** — and the
   field extension is just what is needed here to go from 63 to ~128
   bits of soundness. The remedy collides with the need.
3. **Migrating 28 circuits**, the guardian and the ESPECs. Months.

**Rule for the future**: do not migrate for performance or for
elegance. Migrate only if a need appears that §5.3.bis does not cover —
and then reopen this section, not improvise.

## 7. What this project needs, and is not in the repository

It is said here because it is the honest conclusion of the diagnosis,
and because no seal solves it:

- **External audit**, even partial and of a single circuit. The
  precedent is in `SECURITY.md` §3.1: an under-constraint in production,
  four years unseen, found by audit and not by the project's own suite.
- **A second pair of hands.** All the rigor of this repository comes
  from a single evaluator. The discipline of one does not replace the
  disagreement of two.
- **A narrow, real use case** where ~1,9 TPS and an honest operator are
  *enough*. Systems are adopted for the small case they fit, not for
  the big vision.
- **A second implementation**, even partial and read-only. It is the
  only proof that the protocol contract (`spec/openrpc.json`,
  `spec/vectors/`) is real and not self-referential — and it would find
  errors the project's own gates do not see, because they were written
  by whoever wrote the code.

## 8. How to use this document

- Before accepting a scaling recommendation —from a person, an article
  or an assistant— **check it against §2**. If the problem it says it
  solves does not appear measured in §1, it is not this system's
  problem.
- Before proposing to decentralize, read §3: what is being proposed is
  to **recover two avoided problems**, and that has to be said in the
  same sentence.
- Before answering «would it serve a central bank?», read §6: the short
  answer is that **the single node is not the obstacle**, and that the
  real obstacle —§6.3 and §6.6— is half storage and half institutional.
- If any check of §2 stops giving the written result, this document is
  out of date: **correcting it is part of the change that invalidated
  it.**
